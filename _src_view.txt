1. 明
   M: bright, luminous; clear-sighted
   W: Like Apollo, the bringer of light — clarity that dispels doubt.
   N: Reads open and lucid. Avoid pairing with another light character.
   L: The Book of Songs: shining below, resplendent above.
2. 光
   M: light, radiance; to illumine
   W: The Promethean spark — light carried from the gods to ordinary hands.
   N: Strong and short. Works well as a second character, rarely as a first.
   L: The Book of Changes: inner brilliance waits for the right season to shine.
3. 曜
   M: sunlight; the shining of the sun and stars
   W: Helios driving his chariot — the daily return of light.
   N: Rare and literary. Best for readers who want something uncommon.
   L: The Book of Songs: the fur robe gleams like the sun at daybreak.
4. 曦
   M: the light of the rising sun
   W: Eos, the rosy-fingered dawn of Homer — beginning, not ending.
   N: Very soft and feminine. Strokes are heavy; pair with a simpler character.
   L: Songs of Chu: the sun sinks west, its light still turning back.
5. 晨
   M: morning, daybreak
   W: Aurora opening the gates for the sun — the quiet hour before everything starts.
   N: Warm and unpretentious. Common in modern names without feeling generic.
   L: The Book of Songs: how goes the night? The night draws toward dawn.
6. 昕
   M: dawn, the first light of day
   W: The first crack of light in Genesis — order spoken into darkness.
   N: Rare but easy to read. Good when the surname is already complex.
   L: The Book of Songs: the red bow loosened, joy at the guest who arrives at first light.
7. 灿
   M: brilliant, dazzling
   W: A shield of polished bronze catching the sun — splendor that protects.
   N: Bright and modern. Reads well with single-syllable surnames.
   L: Yueyang Tower: morning glow and evening shade, a thousand moods.
8. 德
   M: virtue, moral power
   W: Arete — excellence of character that others orient themselves by.
   N: Very traditional. Still strong, but pair with a lighter character to stay modern.
   L: The Analects: rule by virtue, and you are the pole star the others turn around.
9. 仁
   M: benevolence, humaneness
   W: Caritas — the goodwill that holds a community together.
   N: Only four strokes, so it balances long surnames beautifully.
   L: The Analects: asked about ren, the Master said — love people.
10. 礼
   M: propriety, ritual respect
   W: Civitas — the courteous forms that let strangers live together.
   N: Dignified. Slightly formal, so it suits adult names rather than childhood ones.
   L: The Analects: to restrain oneself and return to ritual — that is ren.
11. 信
   M: trustworthiness, good faith
   W: Fides — the Roman goddess of good faith, older than any contract.
   N: Blunt and honest. Best as a second character after a softer first one.
   L: The Analects: in dealings with friends, have I been unfaithful?
12. 诚
   M: sincerity, wholeheartedness
   W: Knightly troth — the word given that is never taken back.
   N: Warm and direct. Pairs well with water or wood characters.
   L: The Book of Rites: sincerity is the way of heaven; to be sincere is the way of people.
13. 善
   M: goodness, kindness
   W: The Good beyond being in Plato — what everything aims at without saying so.
   N: Gentle and generous. Slightly feminine-leaning in modern usage.
   L: Tao Te Ching: the highest good is like water, which benefits all things and does not contend.
14. 谦
   M: humility, modesty
   W: Socratic ignorance — wisdom that begins by admitting how little it knows.
   N: Understated. Excellent for a quiet, confident impression.
   L: The Book of Changes: humility succeeds; the noble one carries things through.
15. 毅
   M: resolute, unyielding will
   W: Perseverance of the Stoics — the will that outlasts circumstance.
   N: Firm and masculine. Avoid clustering with other harsh-sounding characters.
   L: The Analects: a scholar must be broad-minded and resolute — the burden is heavy, the road long.
16. 恒
   M: constancy, enduring steadiness
   W: The fixed purpose of Odysseus — the mast bound, the course held.
   N: Plain and dependable. Never sounds dated.
   L: The Analects: a person without constancy in virtue will sooner or later be shamed.
17. 正
   M: upright, correct
   W: Rectitude in the old Roman sense — a line drawn straight.
   N: Very short and crisp. Strong with two-syllable surnames.
   L: The Analects: when the leader is himself upright, the work proceeds without orders.
18. 温
   M: warm, mild, temperate
   W: The tempered heat of a well-made sword — warmth with an edge.
   N: Soft and approachable. Suits both genders, leans feminine in modern usage.
   L: The Analects: the Master was mild yet firm, dignified yet not fierce.
19. 雅
   M: elegant, refined; the classical standard
   W: Grace in the Renaissance sense — proportion that needs no ornament.
   N: Polished and slightly literary. Good for a name meant to sound composed.
   L: The Book of Songs: the ya are the orthodox odes, where the rise and fall of good rule is told.
20. 文
   M: culture, writing, pattern
   W: Humanitas — letters that make a person more fully human.
   N: Only four strokes. One of the safest, most versatile naming characters.
   L: The Analects: when substance and refinement are in balance, there is a noble person.
21. 思
   M: thought, reflection; to long for
   W: Mnemosyne, mother of the muses — memory and thought before speech.
   N: Gentle and introspective. Reads feminine today but has always been neutral.
   L: The Book of Songs: blue, blue the collar — my heart goes on and on.
22. 书
   M: book; to write
   W: The library at Alexandria — knowledge that outlives the hand that wrote it.
   N: Clean and quiet. Very good as a middle character.
   L: Records of the Grand Historian: to probe heaven and humanity, and speak in one voice of my own.
23. 学
   M: to learn; learning
   W: The scholar’s lamp — study as a lifelong practice rather than a stage.
   N: Direct and unpretentious. Slightly earnest, which many families want.
   L: The Analects: to learn and rehearse it in season — is that not a joy?
24. 哲
   M: wise, penetrating in thought
   W: Philosophia — the love of wisdom, not its possession.
   N: Sturdy and masculine. Common enough to be comfortable, rare enough to be distinct.
   L: The Book of Documents: deep wisdom and cultured brightness, mild courtesy truly filled.
25. 睿
   M: astute, far-seeing
   W: Prudentia, the Roman virtue that sees what is coming.
   N: Strong and slightly formal. Works well with a soft first character.
   L: The Book of Documents: reflection is called astuteness; astuteness becomes sageliness.
26. 博
   M: broad, extensive in learning
   W: Encyclopedic curiosity — the Renaissance ideal of knowing widely.
   N: Generous and open. Reads confident rather than intellectual.
   L: The Analects: broaden me with culture, restrain me with ritual.
27. 渊
   M: deep pool; profound
   W: The deep well behind the house — quiet supply that never runs dry.
   N: Quiet and serious. Pairs naturally with water characters.
   L: Tao Te Ching: deep, like the ancestor of all things.
28. 涵
   M: to contain; depth of character; tolerance
   W: The harbor that holds every ship — capacity rather than display.
   N: Very popular in recent decades; comfortable and gently feminine.
   L: History of Song: of deep cultivation, with principle when events press.
29. 聪
   M: quick of hearing; intelligent
   W: Sharp-eared Telemachus — attention as the root of understanding.
   N: Cheerful. Slightly childlike, so consider whether you want it in an adult name.
   L: The Book of Documents: to hear well is called intelligence.
30. 敏
   M: swift, diligent, keen
   W: Alacrity — the readiness that makes discipline look easy.
   N: Light and efficient. Traditionally used for both genders.
   L: The Analects: quick in action, careful in speech.
31. 颖
   M: the tip of an ear of grain; outstanding talent
   W: The blade that shows through the sheath — talent that cannot stay hidden.
   N: Bright and modern. Often paired with 涵 or 慧.
   L: Records of the Grand Historian: had the awl been in the bag sooner, its point would have shown through.
32. 志
   M: aspiration, purpose
   W: The archer’s aim — purpose that selects one target and ignores the rest.
   N: Firm and classic. One of the most durable masculine characters.
   L: The Book of Documents: poetry speaks the purpose; song draws it out.
33. 远
   M: far, distant; far-reaching
   W: Horizon-seeking — the Greek nostos that never settles for the near shore.
   N: Spacious. Works best as a second character.
   L: The Analects: the burden is heavy and the road is far.
34. 宏
   M: grand, vast
   W: The vault of a Roman basilica — a scale that lifts the head.
   N: Broad and confident. Slightly formal in tone.
   L: The Book of Documents: vast as heaven.
35. 鹏
   M: the roc, a legendary bird of immense span
   W: The eagle of Zeus — the flight that sees the whole field at once.
   N: Bold and aspirational. Very masculine in modern usage.
   L: Zhuangzi: the back of the Peng — no one knows how many thousand li it spans.
36. 鸿
   M: the wild goose; great, profound
   W: The migrating goose that keeps its V through weather — loyalty plus reach.
   N: Poised and literary. Also carries the sense of a letter from far away.
   L: Records of the Grand Historian: how should a sparrow know the ambition of the swan?
37. 骏
   M: a fine horse; outstanding
   W: Bucephalus — the horse only one rider could mount.
   N: Energetic and masculine. Pair with a calmer first character.
   L: The Book of Songs: four stallions strong, we drive east.
38. 帆
   M: sail
   W: The sail unfurled — departure taken as a promise.
   N: Light and optimistic. Modern but not trendy.
   L: Li Bai: a time will come to ride the long wind and break the waves.
39. 峰
   M: mountain peak
   W: The summit that is earned by the climb, not granted by the map.
   N: Solid and masculine. Very common, so consider pairing freshly.
   L: Ouyang Xiu: the peak turns, the path bends, and a pavilion spreads its wings above the spring.
40. 岳
   M: high mountain; a sacred peak
   W: Mount Olympus — the height where the air is thin and clear.
   N: Weighty and dignified. Good after a single-syllable surname.
   L: The Book of Songs: the tall sacred peak rises to heaven.
41. 山
   M: mountain
   W: The unmoved mountain of the Psalms — refuge that does not shift.
   N: Only three strokes. Striking and minimal in a two-character name.
   L: The Analects: the wise delight in water, the good delight in mountains.
42. 川
   M: river, stream
   W: Heraclitus’ river — the same and never the same.
   N: Flowing and open. Balances a heavy surname well.
   L: The Analects: standing by the river, the Master said — it goes on like this, day and night.
43. 海
   M: sea
   W: The mare oceanum — the boundary the Romans feared and crossed anyway.
   N: Vast and generous. Very common, so the second character must do more work.
   L: Zhuangzi: of all waters under heaven, none is greater than the sea.
44. 澜
   M: great wave, billow
   W: The wave that carries rather than drowns, if you read the water right.
   N: Slightly dramatic and elegant. Good for a second character.
   L: Mencius: there is an art to watching water — watch where it breaks into waves.
45. 云
   M: cloud
   W: The cloud that takes any shape — freedom without formlessness.
   N: Light and airy. Extremely popular, which cuts both ways.
   L: Yueyang Tower: the bright moon for a thousand li, light leaping on the water.
46. 月
   M: moon; month
   W: Selene — the light that makes the dark navigable.
   N: Quiet and classic. Almost always feminine in modern names.
   L: The Book of Songs: the moon comes out, bright; the lovely one is fair.
47. 星
   M: star
   W: The lodestar — a fixed point used to find the way home.
   N: Bright and friendly. Neutral in gender.
   L: The Book of Songs: those little stars, three and five in the east.
48. 辰
   M: celestial body; the hour; the dragon sign
   W: Kairos — the right moment, seized rather than waited for.
   N: Strong and slightly archaic in a good way. One of the twelve zodiac branches.
   L: The Book of Songs: heaven gave me life — where do my hours fall?
49. 松
   M: pine tree
   W: The evergreen of the northern myths — life that refuses the season.
   N: Steady and masculine. Slightly old-fashioned, which some families prefer.
   L: The Analects: only in the cold of the year do we learn the pine outlasts.
50. 竹
   M: bamboo
   W: The reed of Aesop that bends and survives the storm.
   N: Clean and upright. A quiet, principled impression.
   L: Ode to Bamboo: joints before it breaks ground, still hollow at the height of clouds.
51. 梅
   M: plum blossom
   W: The winter flower of the Persephone myth — beauty timed against the cold.
   N: Classically feminine and dignified. Very readable for foreigners.
   L: Lin Bu: sparse shadows slant across the shallow water; hidden fragrance drifts at dusk.
52. 兰
   M: orchid
   W: The hidden violet of the Odyssey — nobility that grows away from applause.
   N: Refined and gentle. One of the most durable feminine characters.
   L: Songs of Chu: I planted nine acres of orchids and a hundred of basil.
53. 荷
   M: lotus
   W: The lotus of Egyptian cosmology — creation rising from the silt.
   N: Soft and clean. Best for feminine names; reads a little uncommon in English eyes.
   L: On the Love of the Lotus: out of mud without stain, rinsed by clear water without coquetry.
54. 林
   M: forest, grove
   W: The sacred grove of the Druids — many trunks, one canopy.
   N: Easy to pronounce and remember. Neutral gender.
   L: The Book of Songs: the crane stands in the forest, apart from the flock.
55. 静
   M: still, quiet, composed
   W: Hesychia — the stillness of the desert fathers, where noise ends.
   N: Very common in feminine names. Pair with a character that has movement.
   L: Tao Te Ching: reach the extreme of emptiness, hold fast to stillness.
56. 淑
   M: good, virtuous, gentle (of women)
   W: The noble lady of courtly love — worth that is cultivated, not announced.
   N: Traditional and feminine. Reads slightly old-fashioned but with dignity.
   L: The Book of Songs: the modest and good woman — a mate for the noble man.
57. 婉
   M: graceful, agreeable, tactful
   W: The gentle curve of a well-made bow — strength that does not announce itself.
   N: Soft and slightly sweet. Beautiful with wood or water characters.
   L: The Book of Songs: there is a lovely one — clear-browed and graceful.
58. 宁
   M: peaceful, serene
   W: Pax — peace as a condition actively kept, not merely the absence of war.
   N: Short and calm. Neutral but frequently feminine in modern use.
   L: The Book of Songs: when the troubles are settled, there is both safety and peace.
59. 和
   M: harmony, balance
   W: Harmonia — the tuning that makes separate strings sound like one chord.
   N: Warm and balanced. Works for both genders.
   L: The Analects: in the use of ritual, harmony is what matters.
60. 泰
   M: great, peaceful, prosperous
   W: The golden age of Saturn — a season when things hold their place.
   N: Confident and masculine. Slightly formal.
   L: The Book of Changes: Tai — the small departs, the great arrives; auspicious.
61. 安
   M: safe, settled, at ease
   W: The hearth — the small circle of warmth that makes the world manageable.
   N: Very short and friendly. One of the easiest names for foreigners to say.
   L: Tao Te Ching: let them be settled in their homes and glad in their customs.
62. 琳
   M: fine jade
   W: The gem of the lapidary — beauty made by patience and pressure.
   N: Elegant and feminine. Very readable in English contexts.
   L: The Book of Songs: the jade pendants ring against one another as he walks.
63. 瑾
   M: fine jade; also meaning virtue concealed
   W: The buried treasure of the old tales — value that does not need an audience.
   N: Literary and slightly rare. Strong for either gender.
   L: Songs of Chu: I hold jade in my bosom and in my hand, and know not to whom to show it.
64. 瑜
   M: flawless jade; excellence
   W: The pearl of great price — worth that justifies the whole search.
   N: Smooth and feminine. Pairs well with 瑾 for twins.
   L: Songs of Chu: jade held in the bosom, jade held in the hand.
65. 璇
   M: beautiful jade; the second star of the Dipper
   W: Polaris — the star that holds still while everything else turns.
   N: Striking and feminine. A little rare, which suits distinctive names.
   L: The Book of Documents: the jade sighting tube aligned to set the seven governors in order.
66. 琪
   M: fine jade
   W: The reciprocal gift — generosity that expects nothing but returns everything.
   N: Friendly and warm. Common in feminine names without being plain.
   L: The Book of Songs: you gave me a peach; I return a jewel.
67. 瑞
   M: auspicious omen; jade token
   W: The favourable omen of the augurs — a sign read before the journey starts.
   N: Neutral and optimistic. Very common, so pair thoughtfully.
   L: Wang Chong: a rui is the sign of a blessing on its way.
68. 瑶
   M: beautiful jade
   W: The crystal of the fairy tales — clarity that shows only what is there.
   N: Soft and feminine. A gentle, slightly dreamy name.
   L: The Book of Songs: you gave me a peach; I return a jewel.
69. 清
   M: clear, pure
   W: The spring of living water — purity that refreshes rather than excludes.
   N: Extremely versatile. Works in almost any name.
   L: Songs of Chu: when the Canglang water is clear, I may wash my cap-string.
70. 澄
   M: to become clear; transparent
   W: Still water in the sun — depth you can see straight through.
   N: Slightly uncommon. Gives a name a quiet sophistication.
   L: A New Account of the Tales of the World: clarify it and it is not clearer; muddy it and it is not clouded.
71. 朗
   M: bright, clear; open-hearted
   W: The clear sky after a storm — relief you can feel.
   N: Open and masculine. Reads friendly rather than hard.
   L: The Book of Jin: of clear bone and fine bearing, his presence bright and open.
72. 润
   M: to moisten; smooth; to enrich
   W: The oil of anointing — what makes hard things move.
   N: Smooth and understated. Balanced gender lean.
   L: The Book of Changes: wind and rain enrich the world.
73. 熙
   M: bright, prosperous, harmonious
   W: The harvest festival of the old calendar — abundance shared, not hoarded.
   N: Warm and auspicious. On the rise in recent years.
   L: The Book of Songs: the royal army raised, its time of brightness came.
74. 景
   M: bright; prospect, scenery
   W: The prospect from the watchtower — the view earned by climbing.
   N: Confident and slightly formal. Very masculine.
   L: Yueyang Tower: when spring is mild and the light is bright, the waves lie still.
75. 嘉
   M: excellent, praiseworthy, felicitous
   W: The guest-friend of Homeric hospitality — arrival itself as a good thing.
   N: Warm and generous. Excellent first character.
   L: The Book of Songs: I have a fine guest — come, play the strings and pipes.
76. 福
   M: blessing, good fortune
   W: Beatitudo — fortune that includes the ending, not just the peak.
   N: Very direct. Traditional and unambiguous; may read old-fashioned.
   L: The Book of Documents: the five blessings — long life, wealth, health, virtue, and a good end.
77. 荣
   M: glory, honour; to flourish
   W: The laurel crown — honour that is worn, then grows back.
   N: Masculine-leaning. Strong and positive with a slightly competitive edge.
   L: Songs of Chu: green leaves and white blossoms, abundant and lovely.
78. 翔
   M: to soar, to circle in flight
   W: Daedalus’s flight — the freedom that must respect its own limits.
   N: Free and airy. Very masculine in modern usage.
   L: Songs of Chu: high flying, peacefully soaring, riding clear air and the two powers.
79. 衡
   M: balance, measure, to weigh
   W: The scales of the Roman law court — fairness made visible.
   N: Solid and thoughtful. Slightly intellectual.
   L: The Book of Documents: unify the pitch, the measures, the weights.
80. 天
   M: heaven, sky; the natural order
   W: The open sky of the Stoics — order you can trust without seeing its author.
   N: Four strokes, iconic, and every reader knows it. Strong as a first character.
   L: The Book of Songs: heaven gave birth to the people, and with them came measure and law.
81. 宇
   M: the whole space of the world; eaves
   W: The cosmos as a habitable room — the roof that covers everything.
   N: Modern and spacious. Pairs beautifully with water or light characters.
   L: Zhuangzi: what has reality but no fixed place — that is space.
82. 昊
   M: vast and shining (of the sky)
   W: The boundless noon — scale that makes small things honest.
   N: Bold and masculine. Slightly rare, which keeps it memorable.
   L: The Book of Songs: vast is the sky, yet its virtue does not extend.
83. 卓
   M: outstanding, standing tall above
   W: The peak that stands clear of the ridge line — excellence visible from afar.
   N: Crisp and confident. Reads well with almost any surname.
   L: The Analects: I have exhausted my ability, and there it stands, upright before me.
84. 越
   M: to surpass, to cross over
   W: To cross the boundary that was supposed to hold — and be right to.
   N: Energetic. Good as a second character to mark progress.
   L: Zhuangzi: the cook may neglect the kitchen, but the priest does not step over the wine vessels to take his place.
85. 承
   M: to carry, to receive and continue
   W: Tradition in the best sense — the torch handed on still burning.
   N: Warm and grounded. Says "you are trusted with something."
   L: The Book of Changes: to receive heaven and move with the season.
86. 铭
   M: to engrave; an inscription
   W: The inscription cut into stone — a promise that outlasts the hand.
   N: Distinctive and slightly formal. Excellent for a name meant to be remembered.
   L: The Book of Rites: on the bath basin of Tang was engraved — renew yourself daily, again and again.
87. 钦
   M: to respect; reverent
   W: Reverence before the measured order of things — attention as a virtue.
   N: Formal and steady. Reads slightly classic, which suits adult names.
   L: The Book of Documents: reverently follow the vast sky, charting sun, moon and stars.
88. 立
   M: to stand; to establish
   W: The column that holds its own weight — independence without display.
   N: Very short. Striking next to a two-character given name.
   L: The Analects: at thirty, I stood.
89. 达
   M: to reach; open-minded, penetrating
   W: The open road rather than the wall — understanding that travels.
   N: Friendly and unpretentious. Good with a softer first character.
   L: The Analects: Ci is penetrating — what difficulty would government be for him?
90. 通
   M: to connect through; unobstructed
   W: The bridge rather than the gate — connection as the higher skill.
   N: Practical and warm. Rarely used, which makes it stand out.
   L: The Book of Changes: penetrate their changes, and the pattern of heaven and earth completes itself.
91. 元
   M: the first, the origin, the head
   W: Genesis in miniature — the beginning that contains the whole.
   N: Four strokes and very strong. A classic first character.
   L: The Book of Changes: the origin is the head of all that is good.
92. 阳
   M: the sunlit side; yang, the active principle
   W: Solar warmth as a principle, not a mood — the side of things that acts.
   N: Warm and open. Extremely common, so the second character should be choiceful.
   L: The Book of Changes: one yin and one yang — that is called the Way.
93. 永
   M: forever, enduring
   W: The vow that is meant literally — duration as a form of loyalty.
   N: Classic and calm. Works for either gender.
   L: The Book of Songs: not as repayment, but that we may be friends forever.
94. 大
   M: great, vast
   W: Magnanimity — greatness that has no need to be noticed.
   N: Only three strokes. Very bold; usually paired with a dense character.
   L: Tao Te Ching: the greatest sound is scarcely heard; the greatest image has no form.
95. 中
   M: the middle; centered, hitting the mark
   W: The golden mean of Aristotle — the point between two failures.
   N: Simple and balanced. Reads modest, which is its charm.
   L: The Analects: the middle way as a virtue — is it not the highest?
96. 义
   M: righteousness; what is fitting
   W: Justice as a debt owed, not a feeling held.
   N: Only three strokes but very weighty. Powerful with a light first character.
   L: Mencius: life is what I want, and rightness is what I want; if I cannot have both, I give up life for rightness.
97. 敬
   M: respectful, earnest
   W: The attention that treats small things as if they mattered. Because they do.
   N: Dignified. Slightly formal, good for a considered name.
   L: The Book of Songs: be reverent, be reverent — heaven sees clearly.
98. 恭
   M: courteous, respectful in manner
   W: The bow that is not submission but recognition.
   N: Quiet and polite. Rarely used today, so it reads distinctive.
   L: The Analects: courtesy keeps insult away; generosity wins people.
99. 惠
   M: kindness; benefit conferred
   W: Grace as a practical gift — kindness that shows up in the ledger.
   N: Traditionally feminine. Warm without being soft.
   L: The Analects: he nourished the people with kindness and employed them with fairness.
100. 慈
   M: compassion, tender care
   W: The compassion that makes courage possible — love that does not flinch.
   N: Gentle and strong at once. Slightly maternal in tone.
   L: Tao Te Ching: because of compassion, one can be brave.
101. 康
   M: healthy, at ease, prosperous
   W: Salus — wellbeing treated as a public good, not a private mood.
   N: Friendly and optimistic. Very common but never tiresome.
   L: The Book of Documents: the third blessing is health and peace.
102. 宝
   M: treasure, precious thing
   W: The treasure in the field — value discovered rather than displayed.
   N: Very warm and affectionate. A name people smile at.
   L: The Book of Rites: the state of Chu has no treasure but goodness — that is its treasure.
103. 悦
   M: glad, pleased; to delight in
   W: Joy as a by-product of doing the right thing — not a goal in itself.
   N: Bright and friendly. Modern favourite, but reads sincere.
   L: Mencius: how rightness delights my heart, as rich food delights my mouth.
104. 欣
   M: glad, rejoicing
   W: The lifting of spirits that a good meal and good company can produce.
   N: Open and cheerful. Works well as a first character.
   L: The Book of Songs: the good wine brings gladness, the roasted meat gives fragrance.
105. 怡
   M: harmonious, pleasant, at ease
   W: Serenity that is chosen rather than found — comfort with one’s own company.
   N: Soft and comfortable. Very popular in feminine names.
   L: Shuowen: yi means harmony.
106. 恬
   M: quiet, content, undisturbed
   W: Ataraxia — the untroubled calm of the Epicurean garden.
   N: Gentle and rare. Reads thoughtful rather than passive.
   L: Zhuangzi: those of old who governed by the Way used stillness to nourish understanding.
107. 慧
   M: wisdom, discernment
   W: Sophia — wisdom as a person, not a quantity.
   N: Common in feminine names. Strong and traditional.
   L: Shuowen: hui means quick-witted.
108. 芸
   M: a fragrant herb; also "numerous"
   W: The herb grown not for show but for its use — quiet usefulness.
   N: Very elegant and slightly literary. Popular in modern names.
   L: The Book of Songs: to the left, the noble one fits there; to the right, the noble one holds it.
109. 芷
   M: angelica; a fragrant plant of the marshes
   W: The flower of the Odyssey picked before nightfall — beauty that must be gathered.
   N: Refined and distinctive. A little literary for everyday use, which is its appeal.
   L: Songs of Chu: I wore wild ginger and angelica, and strung autumn orchids as pendants.
110. 莲
   M: lotus
   W: The lotus of the Nile creation myth — order rising out of silt.
   N: Soft and traditional. Pairs well with water characters.
   L: On the Love of the Lotus: from mud unstained, washed in clear water without coquetry.
111. 薇
   M: a wild vetch eaten by soldiers on campaign
   W: The plant that grows where the army passes — endurance recorded in something small.
   N: Very popular and very readable. Gentle and slightly florid.
   L: The Book of Songs: when I went away, the willows swayed; now I return, sleet falls thick.
112. 露
   M: dew
   W: The transient bead of dawn — beauty that is honest about its hour.
   N: Delicate but heavy in strokes. Needs a simple second character.
   L: The Book of Songs: in the field the creepers grow, and the dew lies round.
113. 晴
   M: clear after rain
   W: The clearing sky after the storm — relief with no effort in it.
   N: Bright and open. Very friendly for feminine names.
   L: Cui Hao: on the clear river the Hanyang trees stand distinct; rank grasses fill the Parrot Isle.
114. 岩
   M: rock, cliff
   W: The rock of the abbey — what remains when weather has had its way.
   N: Hard and confident. Very masculine; balances a softer second character.
   L: The Book of Songs: the southern hills rise steep, and the stones stand sheer.
115. 桐
   M: paulownia tree
   W: The tree cut for music — usefulness that becomes something better than itself.
   N: Quiet and upright. Rare enough to feel distinct.
   L: The Book of Songs: catalpa and paulownia, lacquer and cypress — cut to make lutes and zithers.
116. 洛
   M: the River Luo; a great river name
   W: A river that made a civilisation — place as destiny.
   N: Smooth and modern. Popular for both genders.
   L: The Book of Songs: look at the Luo — how vast and deep the water runs.
117. 沛
   M: abundant rain; vigorous
   W: The storm that ends the drought — plenty arriving faster than expected.
   N: Energetic and open. Neutral gender.
   L: Mencius: heaven gathers the clouds, and the rain comes down in abundance.
118. 舟
   M: boat
   W: Charon’s skiff turned toward the future instead — passage as a choice.
   N: Small and clear. Reads calm rather than adventurous.
   L: The Book of Songs: drifting that cypress boat, drifting on the current.
119. 溪
   M: mountain stream
   W: The brook that always finds the sea — persistence without particular force.
   N: Soft and feminine-leaning. A gentle, unforced name.
   L: Tao Te Ching: the Way in the world is as valleys and streams to the rivers and seas.
120. 泓
   M: deep clear water
   W: The deep pool whose surface is quiet — reserve rather than absence.
   N: Clean and uncommon. Reads capable, not showy.
   L: The Book of Songs: how vast and deep the water — depth is what it has to offer.

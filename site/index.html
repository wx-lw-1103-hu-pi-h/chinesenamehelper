Why this is not a transliteration tool

Most Chinese name generators take your English name and sound it out in Chinese characters. The result is pronounceable and almost never a name. This one works the other way around.

历

Your birth date sets the frame

The date is converted to the Chinese lunar calendar, giving your zodiac animal and the eight characters of your chart. The five elements are counted, and names lean toward whatever is thinnest.

意

Your words choose the meaning

Describe yourself as calm, driven, curious or gentle, and the engine matches those qualities to characters that carry them — not to characters that sound like your name.

典

Every character has a source

Each choice is backed by a real classical line — the Book of Songs, the Analects, the Songs of Chu — with the original text, its English rendering, and a Western parallel.

书

A signature, not just a name

Every result generates calligraphy in four styles with a seal, plus stroke-by-stroke animation so you can actually learn to write what you were given.

Questions

Is this a translation of my English name?

No. Transliteration produces names like 麦可 for Michael — they sound Chinese but read like a brand label to a native speaker. This tool picks characters that fit your birth date and the qualities you describe, so you get something a Chinese family might actually choose.

How exactly does the birthday affect the name?

The date becomes a lunar date, which gives your zodiac animal and the eight characters of your birth chart. The tool counts the five elements — wood, fire, earth, metal, water — and leans toward characters supplying the thinnest one.

This is the simplified version of the traditional rule. It is a naming heuristic with a long history, not a full consultation, and we say so rather than dressing it up.

Do I get a surname as well?

Yes, if you want one. Pick any of the hundred most common Chinese surnames and the given name is designed to sit well after it. Leave it blank and you get a two-character given name on its own.

Where do the meanings and quotations come from?

Every character carries a real citation from a canonical text, the line it appears in, an English rendering, and a Western parallel so the meaning lands even without Chinese.

Can I keep the name, print it, or share it?

Yes. Each name exports as a printable card, each character can be downloaded as a stroke-practice sheet, and your result set is encoded in the page address so a bookmark restores exactly what you saw.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The lunar conversion, the element count, the name selection and the calligraphy rendering all happen in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded or stored.

What if I don't like any of the names?

Press Another set. The engine is deterministic, so the same inputs always give the same result — the button changes the seed rather than rerunning a random draw, which means you can come back to a set you liked.

Stroke order

Click a square to replay that character. The pale outline is the target shape; the dark stroke is the current one.

Five rules that cover most characters

Stroke order is not memorised character by character — a handful of rules handles the majority, and the animation shows where the exceptions lie.

先横后竖Horizontal before vertical. 十 is written across, then down.
先撇后捺Left-falling before right-falling. 人 opens out from the left.
从上到下Top to bottom. 三 is written from the top bar down.
从左到右Left to right. 川 moves across, stroke by stroke.
先外后内Outside before inside, and the closing stroke last. 国 finishes with its bottom edge.

Why it matters

Why does stroke order matter if the finished character looks the same?

Three practical reasons. Handwriting speed and legibility both depend on it — a character written in the wrong order tends to come out lopsided at speed. Reading cursive depends on it, because joined strokes only make sense in the standard sequence. And dictionary lookup by stroke count assumes you know the order.

What order are strokes written in?

Top to bottom, left to right, horizontal before vertical, outside before inside, and the closing stroke of an enclosure last. The animation shows the exact sequence for each character rather than asking you to apply the rules by eye.

Can I print a practice sheet?

Yes — the print button lays out the characters in a grid with the pale target and empty cells beside each, which is the standard worksheet format used in Chinese primary schools.

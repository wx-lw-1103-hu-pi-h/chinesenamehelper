A name on the cup you use

Which characters work here

Up to 16 strokes work on a hand-thrown cup, because the surface is large enough to give dense characters room. This is the carrier where the library’s more elaborate characters — 澜, 霖, 霜 — stop being a problem.

Why it is laid out this way

The name sits on the vertical face of the cup, not around the rim: a curved inscription loses its first and last character to the curve. On a scoop or a caddy lid it lies flat along the length, which is the easiest surface of any carrier here.

Before you commit

Glaze matters more than size. An unglazed clay body takes a carved or pressed name that survives firing; a glazed cup needs the name added as a decal or a brush-written mark under the glaze, which changes what the finished piece can look like.

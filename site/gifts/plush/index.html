Soft, and still readable

A plush is the carrier with the most personality and the least surface discipline. One character, embroidered large, survives the softness better than a full name stitched small.

Which characters work here

Up to 14 strokes embroider cleanly. Diagonals soften on a stuffed body because the filling pushes the fabric outward, so squarer characters hold their shape better than sprawling ones.

Why it is laid out this way

A single character, centred on a flat panel — a paw pad, a belly patch, a cushion face. Curved surfaces pull the stitch line as the toy is stuffed, so the panel has to be embroidered before it is sewn in, not after.

Before you commit

This is the carrier where safety and materials matter most: a plush intended for a child is subject to toy safety rules in most markets, and the embroidery thread is part of that assessment, not an afterthought.

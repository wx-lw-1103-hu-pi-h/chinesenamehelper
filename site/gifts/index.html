Your name, on something you keep

A Chinese name is a design, not just a label. These are the objects people actually put one on — and what each object demands of the characters you chose.

Every carrier below is judged by the same test: can the character still be read at that size? That is why a seal page and a scroll page recommend different names from the same library.

On choosing a carrier

The seal is the oldest and the most Chinese of these: a carved stamp that turns a name into a signature. It is also the least forgiving, because a stone the size of a thumbnail has to hold the whole name.

The pendant, the fan and the scroll each buy back room in a different direction — depth on a neck, height on a leaf, area on paper. That is the whole trade-off, and it is why this site sorts carriers by how many strokes they tolerate rather than by price.

Nothing here is sold directly. These pages describe what works and what does not; the enquiry form on each one puts you in touch about a specific piece.
